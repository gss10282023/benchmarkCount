# AndroidWorld pre-outcome checklist lock v3

This checklist is drafted and locked before any concrete agent outcome or per-record released-evaluator result is available. Treat the supplied case packet as the sole case-specific input. Do not infer, request, or refer to any agent trajectory, run success/failure, released-evaluator label, evidence score, or benchmark-conflict decision.

- State `native.user_goal` from the official task/user intent.
- Define the native criterion from the released evaluator/oracle and official runner semantics, but formulate `native.decisive_artifacts`, `success_if`, `fail_if`, and `undecided_if` so that the later Scorer can decide S/F/U independently from retained underlying execution evidence.
- A recorded `native_label`, `native_score`, `is_successful` scalar, released-evaluator output, episode success field, or equivalent summary result is non-decisive metadata. It must not be a decisive artifact and must not itself establish S or F. Use the underlying evaluator-visible state, completion gate, trace, database, filesystem, message, or other retained artifact instead.
- A retained `done=false` may establish native failure where the official runner formally gates it to failure. Missing evidence, evaluator/runtime exceptions, NaN, or the absence of a usable underlying observation are U unless other retained evidence independently establishes the native criterion or its negation.
- Keep official task or policy requirements that exceed the evaluator/oracle's native criterion out of the native rules. Add them to `stronger.additional_conditions` only when they have concrete, case-specific official source support and are reviewable from realistically retainable evidence.
- Review the official dispatched/runtime user goal against every native evaluator comparison and enumerate all concrete supported gaps, not just one representative gap. In particular, record task-required exact strings/values, answer formats, item uniqueness/completeness, ordering, named-app/provenance steps, or explicit action sequence when the native evaluator uses fuzzy/tolerant comparison or checks only an end state. Multiple supported conditions are allowed when needed.
- Do not turn a general desire for better evidence into a stronger outcome requirement. For information-retrieval tasks, do not require the app's final state to remain equal to a precomputed answer unless the official dispatched goal or policy explicitly requires non-modification or final-state persistence.
- Do not invent an initial-state precondition, state transition, extra action, or app-use requirement merely because the goal uses an action verb. A verify task that officially starts in the requested state does not imply that the agent must first change away from that state. Require a particular procedure, provenance, or ordering only when the dispatched user goal explicitly says so.
- Use the actual dispatched/runtime goal for this case as user intent. Do not promote a conflicting unused metadata template, implementation typo, alternate template, target-construction detail, or evaluator serialization detail into a stronger condition. Such inconsistencies are not stronger requirements and benchmark-conflict judgment is out of scope here.
- Exactness conditions must track what the official dispatched goal concretely specifies. Do not require incidental trailing newlines, storage encodings, database IDs, timestamps, or other representation details unless the goal or policy explicitly requires them.
- Prefer no stronger condition to any subjective, stylistic, generic, speculative, hidden-state, counterfactual, or extra-judge requirement.
- Do not make or encode a benchmark-conflict judgment. Conflict is a later, separate record-level review that requires retained artifacts and source pointers; neither native success nor stronger failure is sufficient by itself.
- If a model-visible compact packet lists a Source Inventory path but omits that path's full body from `Packet Source Files`, do not cite or rely on the omitted body. Use only embedded official source bodies and other embedded packet material.
